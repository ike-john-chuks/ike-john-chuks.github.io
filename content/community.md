Giving my time, experience, and support back to every community I find myself in has always been a meaningful way to contribute. I may not have all the funds I desire in my bank account, but I have time, and I use it wisely to serve in the following ways.

## Visually Impaired Unit

<p class="eyebrow">2019–2021 · Enugu, Nigeria</p>

After graduating from the Federal University of Technology in Owerri, I worked at the College of the Immaculate Conception, Enugu, Nigeria, as support staff to the Proprietor, Rev. Hilary Mgbodile. While there, I saw an opportunity to support the department of visually impaired students.

I installed JAWS screen-reading software on about five personal computers to help the students learn to use a computer. With this software, they were able to complete basic tasks such as reading documents and browsing the internet for academic information.

## LoveKocani, North Macedonia

<p class="eyebrow">2025</p>

During my master's studies, I learned about the tragic fire in Kocani, North Macedonia. A friend asked if I could help complete a website for a fundraising campaign supporting bereaved families.

I created a fully functional website on Wix and helped raise public awareness on the streets of Skopje by handing out invitation fliers and sharing our vision for support. Through this collaborative effort, we raised about 220,000 MKD and distributed the support to affected homes.

[Visit the LoveKocani website →](https://www.sakajgokocani.org/)

## Camp Bogilak, North Macedonia

<p class="eyebrow">2025, 2026</p>

Children with special needs are dear to me, and whenever an opportunity to help arises, I take it. I was privileged to take part in two camps with a group in Macedonia called Camp Bogilak. Each camp lasts a week, bringing together children with special needs and their parents for games, workshops, sports, and other activities.

At my first camp, I was assigned to support Daniel, a child with a very special character. During my second camp, I supported Maxim. Across these experiences, I spent 14 days at camp, including seven days serving, and learned how to care for children with special needs. I left with a sense of fulfillment and gratitude for having been able to give back.

## YoungLife, Macedonia

<p class="eyebrow">2024–2026</p>

With YoungLife in Macedonia, I served in many capacities and shared many moments of joy and laughter. Two experiences stood out: the camp in Krani and my Bible study with teenage boys.

At the Krani camp, I was assigned seven boys whom I spent a week nurturing and serving. I accompanied them to games, workshops, breakfast, lunch, and dinner.

The Bible study grew out of a camp in Gevgelia as a way to stay close to God and our religious values. Afterwards, I often met with the boys on Saturdays and Sundays to study the Bible and pray together.

## Football in Voydan, North Macedonia

<p class="eyebrow">2024–2026</p>

Football in Voydan began with a desire to help young boys learn to love one another. One Sunday, while passing a public playground in Karposh 2, North Macedonia, I noticed a group of boys playing football. The game did not seem to be going well. I stepped in as a stranger and was not initially welcomed, which was understandable.

I kept returning, mostly on Saturdays and Sundays, and gradually became friends with some of the children. In time, I met many of their parents, who gave me permission to work with their children.

Together, we made progress in several areas:

- **Structure and responsibility:** We introduced three-hour sessions so the boys could go home and attend to other chores, instead of playing until dark.
- **Managing conflict:** I encouraged the boys to bring disagreements to the referee—me—and, once a dispute was resolved, to shake hands and forgive one another.
- **Caring for shared spaces:** Because we use a public playground, everyone took part in picking up litter after the game before leaving.

These are just a few of the highlights from an experience that has meant a great deal to me.
